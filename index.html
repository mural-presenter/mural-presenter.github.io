Multi-Agent Units of Responsibility for Authoring Long-Horizon Presentations

Page ownership that carries through authoring, inspection, and revision. MURAL coordinates an HTML presentation through shared facts, design decisions, and persistent responsibility.

Abstract

Automated presentation generation requires accurate content, coherent narratives, and consistent visual semantics across slides. However, whole-deck authoring faces a growing context burden as execution histories accumulate, while independent per-slide authoring struggles to coordinate cross-slide dependencies. To address this, we introduce MuralPresenter, a multi-agent framework for long-horizon authoring of editable HTML presentations. The framework maintains a persistent shared deck state and adaptively assigns production responsibility to single-slide or slide-group agents based on joint authoring and revision needs. Each agent retains responsibility for generating, inspecting, and revising its assigned slides.

We adopt selective action supervision for model fine-tuning, retaining historical context while supervising only assistant messages newly generated within each execution segment. Our evaluation covers presentation generation, multi-turn editing, and requirement fulfillment across authoring stages.

With score-based output selection, MuralPresenter-27B obtains the highest PresentBench Overall score among the listed systems. The 9B model reaches 90.21% content accuracy on SlidesGen-Bench. On long decks, adaptive assignment improves mean final quality by 2.49 points over whole-deck production, with greater workload and a mean score comparable to per-slide assignment.

The MURAL approach

Shared deck state. Responsibility where it belongs.

The Orchestrator maintains deck plans and assignments; Research and Image supply evidence and assets. With those inputs shared, unit-adaptive assignment gives independent pages to Single Owners and pages needing joint adjustment to one Group Owner. Ownership persists through authoring, inspection, and revision.

Keep the context. Supervise the new actions.

Supervision policies and controls

Full

Supervise historical and current assistant actions.

Global Selective

Keep history as input but mask its assistant loss; supervise current target actions across roles.

Role-wise Selective

Apply that mask only to Slide segments as an ablation condition.

Controls

The 9,000 units are execution segments, not tasks or decks. Historical imports and separately collected artifact-first recovery trajectories are excluded. Within each backbone, record membership, order, packing, and hyperparameters are fixed. Training updates the language backbone while freezing the vision encoder and merger.

ThreadEval: trace the process. Inspect the deck.

Final artifacts show what a system delivers; process evidence helps locate omissions and handoff problems. ThreadEval uses task-specific rubrics to trace requirements through intermediate artifacts and execution logs, reporting Process and Final separately.

35 Chinese / English tasks · 6 domains · 20–40 slides per deck

Process · Where were requirements fulfilled?

Trace intermediate artifacts and execution logs through research and transfer (R), executable plans (P), asset preparation and use (I), and page realization and inspection (S). In the compass case: did research findings enter the plan and reach the assigned Owners?

Final · Did the deck deliver?

Inspect eight Deck dimensions and five Page dimensions. In the compass case: were all 40 slides delivered, including the requested comparison table?

How is Final calculated?Deck-level quality contributes 70%; page-level quality contributes 30%. Process is reported separately, without a combined overall score.

What do the results tell us?

01 / Generation

Content gains do not imply uniform visual gains.

The 9B and 27B models reach 90.21% and 94.92% Content on SlidesGen-Bench. The 27B model scores 8.42 on colorfulness-based Engagement. It does not lead Total Aesthetics, and Harmony falls relative to its untuned backbone.

The 72.6 PresentBench score includes score-conditioned output selection. Generation and recovery opportunities differ, so it does not establish a fixed-budget SFT gain or improved reliability.

02 / Process and delivery

Better execution need not mean a better final deck.

In the ThreadEval archive, Process improves by 9.13 points for 9B and 2.05 for 27B. For 27B, research transfer rises from 78.27 to 87.94, while plan realization and inspection falls from 89.26 to 76.03. An aggregate gain can coexist with weaker stages.

The archive includes formal and diagnostic evaluations and must be interpreted separately from strict Final-only supervision results.

03 / Multi-turn editing

Five completed edits test continuity of the artifact.

The 27B model completes five edits on all 294 tasks. Trained and untuned models improve mean metrics over their initial decks; between-model confidence intervals include zero.

Each turn imports previous artifacts into a fresh workspace. This does not establish a benefit from persistent Owner history.

04 / Responsibility

Distributing production helps; extra grouping gains remain uncertain.

On 35 production tasks with fixed preparation and tools, per-page and adaptive ownership gain 2.40 and 2.49 Final points over whole-deck ownership, with greater recorded workload.

Adaptive ownership exceeds per-page ownership by only 0.09 points (95% CI [−1.35, 1.60]); no extra advantage is established. Usage includes continuations, excludes shared preparation, and is a lower bound when failed-call usage is missing.

05 / Supervision

Selective supervision reduces recorded input workload.

Versus Full, Global Selective records 38.0% fewer input tokens and 30.2% fewer turns. Its +2.20 Final difference (95% CI [−0.22, 4.57]) means neither a quality gain nor non-inferiority is established.

Global exceeds Vanilla by 4.02 Final points, but also uses more total tokens and turns. Historical search, image backends, and quotas differ, preventing a pure training-effect interpretation. Completed-chain accounting is not full-campaign cost or elapsed time.
