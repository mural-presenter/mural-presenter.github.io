MuralPresenter: Dependency-Aware Multi-Agent Authoring for Long-Horizon Presentations

Shared knowledge, blueprints, and assets coordinate the deck. Persistent Slide Owners carry related slides through generation, inspection, and revision; quality-aware trajectory distillation teaches effective authoring actions.

Abstract

Long-horizon presentation authoring requires carrying content into slides while maintaining consistency throughout generation and revision. Existing workflows primarily organize authoring stages, often overlooking how production responsibility should be assigned. We propose MuralPresenter, which splits slide production among local Owners under shared deck coordination. Each Owner retains responsibility through generation, inspection, and revision. To enhance authoring capabilities, we introduce quality-aware trajectory distillation, combining high-quality trajectory selection with selective supervision to learn effective authoring behaviors. MuralPresenter-27B achieves the highest Overall score on PresentBench and Content accuracy on SlidesGen-Bench, two public benchmarks.

The MURAL approach

Shared deck state. Responsibility where it belongs.

The Orchestrator maintains deck plans and assignments; Research and Asset supply evidence and assets. With those inputs shared, unit-adaptive assignment gives independent pages to Single Owners and pages needing joint adjustment to one Group Owner. Ownership persists through authoring, inspection, and revision.

Learn effective actions from quality-reviewed trajectories.

Quality-aware trajectory distillation reviews both the delivered deck and the utility of current actions. Historical actions and tool feedback remain as context; only approved current actions receive selective supervision. The training corpus contains 9,000 execution segments from 686 tasks, used to train the 9B and 27B models.

For example, enlarging a bird photograph may obscure a compass legend. The flawed edit and rendering feedback remain as context; the approved correction becomes the learning target. A good final deck does not make every earlier action worth imitating.

Supervision policies and controls

Full

Supervise historical and current assistant actions.

Global Selective

Keep history as input but mask its assistant loss; supervise current target actions across roles.

Role-wise Selective

Apply that mask only to Slide segments as an ablation condition.

Controls

The 9,000 units are execution segments, not tasks or decks. Historical imports and separately collected artifact-first recovery trajectories are excluded. Within each backbone, record membership, order, packing, and hyperparameters are fixed. Training updates the language backbone while freezing the vision encoder and merger.

ThreadEval: trace the process. Inspect the deck.

Final artifacts show what a system delivers; process evidence helps locate omissions and handoff problems. ThreadEval uses task-specific rubrics to trace requirements through intermediate artifacts and execution logs, reporting Process and Overall separately.

35 Chinese / English tasks · 6 domains · 20–40 slides per deck

Process · Where were requirements fulfilled?

Trace intermediate artifacts and execution logs through research and transfer (R), executable plans (P), asset preparation and use (I), and page realization and inspection (S). In the compass case: did research findings enter the plan and reach the assigned Owners?

Quality · Did the deck deliver?

Inspect eight Deck dimensions and five Page dimensions. In the compass case: were all 40 slides delivered, including the requested comparison table?

What do the results tell us?

01 / Generation

Content gains do not imply uniform visual gains.

The 9B and 27B models reach 90.21% and 94.92% Content on SlidesGen-Bench. The 27B model scores 8.42 on colorfulness-based Engagement. It does not lead Total Aesthetics, and Harmony falls relative to its untuned backbone.

The 27B model leads the listed systems on PresentBench Overall at 72.6. Published systems retain their source protocols; this comparison does not establish equal computational cost.

02 / Process and delivery

Trace where requirements are lost between plans and slides.

On ThreadEval, the 27B model scores 79.04 on presentation Overall and 87.75 on Process, the highest Process score in the main comparison. For 9B, planning (P) reaches 83.60, while asset use (I) and slide realization (S) reach only 36.13 and 33.60, revealing a gap between planning and delivery.

The archive includes formal and diagnostic evaluations and must be interpreted separately from strictly validated ablations.

03 / Multi-turn editing

Five completed edits test continuity of the artifact.

The 27B model completes five edits on all 294 tasks. Trained and untuned models improve mean metrics over their initial decks; between-model confidence intervals include zero.

Each turn imports previous artifacts into a fresh workspace. This does not establish a benefit from persistent Owner history.

04 / Responsibility

Distributing production helps; extra grouping gains remain uncertain.

On 35 production tasks with fixed preparation and tools, per-page and adaptive ownership gain 2.40 and 2.49 Overall points over whole-deck ownership, with greater recorded workload.

Adaptive ownership exceeds per-page ownership by only 0.09 points (95% CI [−1.35, 1.60]); no extra advantage is established. Usage includes continuations, excludes shared preparation, and is a lower bound when failed-call usage is missing.

05 / Supervision

Selective supervision reduces recorded input workload.

Versus Full, Global Selective records 38.0% fewer input tokens and 30.2% fewer turns. Its +2.20 Overall difference (95% CI [−0.22, 4.57]) means neither a quality gain nor non-inferiority is established.

Global exceeds Vanilla by 4.02 Overall points, but also uses more total tokens and turns. Usage counts cover completed runs; elapsed time and monetary cost were not measured.
